# Experiments of 30 September 2026

Three experiments on two questions:

1. **Do the RealNVP coupling layers help?** On TSP-20, the flow is replaced by a plain diagonal Gaussian over random keys, and everything else is kept the same.
2. **When does the learned distribution keep several minima, and when does it collapse to one?** This is tested on a 2D toy problem and then with a fixed-temperature (Boltzmann) objective on TSP-20.

All numbers below are computed from the committed CSVs. There are 10 seeds (42–51) per configuration unless stated otherwise.

## Summary

- **The coupling layers do not measurably help on TSP-20.** A diagonal Gaussian `y = μ + σ·z` (40 parameters) finds the optimum about as often as RealNVP: 7/10 vs 8/10 with KL, and 3/10 vs 4/10 without. With KL it finds the optimum 3–5× earlier. **Caveat:** the Gaussian learning rate was tuned and the RealNVP one (1e-4) was not.
- **Collapse is not specific to the flow.** The Gaussian collapses into the same near-optimal tour (3.522437) as RealNVP.
- **Without a temperature term the distribution always ends in a single minimum.** The objective `E[f]` is minimized by a point mass, and training drains the worst basin first. An empty basin produces no samples and therefore no gradient, so it never comes back.
- **A fixed temperature keeps several minima.** With the free energy `E[f] + T·E[log q]`, the optimum becomes the Boltzmann distribution `∝ exp(−f/T)`. The flow matches it closely in 2D when Δf/T ≤ 1, keeps all basins with a bias at Δf/T = 2–3, and loses the worse basin at Δf/T = 4.
- **On TSP-20 a fixed temperature increases diversity but does not concentrate mass on the optimum.** This refutes the prediction made before the run.

## 1. TSP-20: RealNVP vs diagonal Gaussian random keys

**Question:** does the flow architecture (coupling layers, dependencies between keys, multimodality) contribute anything, or would a simple Gaussian search distribution do the same?

**Setup:** `permutation/tsp/gaussian_tsp20_trace.py` is a copy of `realnvp_tsp20_trace.py` in which only the model changes: `y = μ + σ·z` with μ = 0 and σ = 1 at initialization. The training loop, REINFORCE advantage, annealed KL, validation, checkpoint and test are identical (checked with diff). Instance, budget and protocol are those of the TSP-20 trace runs: batch 1024, 2000 epochs, 2,216,960 evaluations including validation.

With LR 1e-4 a Gaussian can barely move (μ changes by at most ~0.2 in 2000 epochs), so its learning rate was chosen by a sweep over {1e-3, 3e-3, 1e-2, 3e-2} on separate seeds (0–2). The rule was lowest mean best-ever length, with ties broken by mean test length. The chosen values are **1e-2 without KL** (tied with 3e-3 on best-ever, better test mean) and **3e-2 with KL** (the edge of the grid; 2/3 hits in the sweep).

| | RealNVP, no KL | Gaussian, no KL | RealNVP + KL | Gaussian + KL |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Learning rate | 1e-4 | 1e-2 (tuned) | 1e-4 | 3e-2 (tuned) |
| Runs that found the optimum 3.513669 | 4/10 | 3/10 | 8/10 | 7/10 |
| Mean best-ever length | 3.5786 | 3.5198 | 3.5154 | 3.5163 |
| First hit, evaluations (min–max) | 370k–498k | 196k–494k | 403k–799k | 122k–160k |
| Mean test length (final generator) | 3.6955 | 3.8487 | 3.5229 | 3.5279 |
| Unique tours in 16384 test samples | 2–8 | 23–748 | 7–20 | 1–26 |
| Seeds whose generator samples the optimum | 0 | 1 (1.1 %) | 0 | 3 (≥ 99.9 %) |

Paired per seed (Gaussian vs RealNVP):

| | Best-ever length (better / tie / worse) | Test mean length (better / worse) |
|---|---:|---:|
| No KL | 2 / 6 / 2 | 3 / 7 |
| KL | 1 / 7 / 2 | 9 / 1 |

- Without KL, the Gaussian σ diverges (final mean σ 537–14040), so its generator stays broad and its test mean is worse.
- With KL, the Gaussian has a better test mean on 9/10 seeds. The exception is seed 44, which has not converged (σ 0.83, test mean 3.603) and raises the Gaussian average.
- On 3 seeds the Gaussian + KL generator samples the optimum almost exclusively. RealNVP + KL does this on no seed, and always ends on 3.522437.

**Conclusion:** on TSP-20 the coupling layers bring no measurable benefit over a diagonal Gaussian. A fair "the flow cannot help" claim still needs a RealNVP learning-rate sweep (1e-4, 3e-4, 1e-3, 3e-3 on seeds 0–2), because only the Gaussian was tuned.

Figures: `results/permutation_optimization/anytime/tsp20_gaussian_{kl,no_kl}_convergence.{png,pdf}` show best-so-far vs evaluations for (1+1)-EA, RealNVP and Gaussian (median and 25–75 percentile band).

## 2. 2D toy study: when does the flow keep several minima?

**Setup:** `continuous_optimization/basin_2d_trace.py` (two basins) and `basin_2d_multi_trace.py` (four basins).

- **Objective:** a minimum of quadratic bowls, evaluated on a 50×50 grid in [−1, 1]². The basin minima sit at (±0.5, ±0.5) with f values 0, Δf, ….
- **Model:** RealNVP with 4 coupling layers, batch 1024, 2000 epochs, fixed LR 1e-4, no plateau restore.
- **Losses:**
  - Without T, the loss is standardized REINFORCE on f.
  - With T, each sample's cost is `f + T·log q`, centered only, which minimizes the free energy `E[f] + T·E[log q] = T·KL(q ‖ p_T) + const` with `p_T ∝ exp(−f/T)`.
- **Measurement:** 100k fresh samples at the last epoch. A basin counts as occupied if it holds at least 1 % of the mass (threshold fixed in advance). The Boltzmann target is computed exactly on the grid.

### Without temperature: always one minimum

| Δf (right − left) | Both basins kept | Only left | Only right |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.2 | 0/10 | 10/10 | 0/10 |
| 0 | 3/10 | 3/10 | 4/10 |

- **Δf = 0.2:** the worse basin drops below 1 % between epochs 100 and 140 on every seed.
- **Δf = 0 (no preference in the objective):**
  - Which side wins is not predicted by the epoch-0 mass. For example, seed 43 started with 55 % on the right and ended entirely on the left, while seed 46 started with 45 % on the right and ended entirely on the right. The outcome is mostly decided by epoch ~200.
  - The bimodal seeds reach a *lower* mean f (0.0135–0.0152) than the collapsed ones (0.0197–0.0202). The flow has a density cap: `tanh` on the scale bounds the log-determinant, so each basin has a minimum width of about 0.139. Collapse is therefore a worse outcome that the training dynamics end up in, not the optimum.

### With temperature, two basins (Δf = 0.2)

| T | Δf/T | Target mass in right basin | Final right mass, median (min–max) | Both basins kept |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1.0 | 0.2 | 0.416 | 0.417 (0.413–0.421) | 10/10 |
| 0.5 | 0.4 | 0.374 | 0.373 (0.372–0.380) | 10/10 |
| 0.3 | 0.67 | 0.319 | 0.319 (0.314–0.322) | 10/10 |
| 0.2 | 1 | 0.256 | 0.253 (0.240–0.257) | 10/10 |
| 0.1 | 2 | 0.116 | 0.103 (0.094–0.109) | 10/10 |
| 0.05 | 4 | 0.018 | 0.0002 (0.00004–0.0003) | 0/10 |

- **T = 0.1:** the right mass first dips (minimum 0.02–0.10 per seed, between epochs 260 and 860) and then slowly recovers. It has not converged by epoch 2000.
- **T = 0.05:** the right basin drops below 1 % at epochs 140–340 and below 0.1 % at epochs 260–760, and never recovers.

Figure: `continuous_optimization/basin2d_delta0.2_temperature_p_right.{png,pdf}`. Summary table: `basin2d_delta0.2_temperature_summary.csv`.

### Four basins (f = 0, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3)

| T | All 4 basins kept | Mean total-variation distance to target | Worst basin (f = 0.3): target / mean final |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| none | 0/10 (10/10 only global) | – | – / 0 |
| 1.0 | 10/10 | 0.0025 | 0.191 / 0.191 |
| 0.5 | 10/10 | 0.0034 | 0.163 / 0.163 |
| 0.3 | 10/10 | 0.0044 | 0.130 / 0.129 |
| 0.2 | 10/10 | 0.0081 | 0.095 / 0.094 |
| 0.1 | 10/10 | 0.0203 | 0.031 / 0.025 |

- **Without T, the basins die in order of f.** Mass drops below 1 % in the f = 0.3 basin at epochs 60–80, the f = 0.2 basin at 100–140, and the f = 0.1 basin at 100–160, regardless of the initial mass.
- **With T, the fit is close.** At T = 1 the total-variation distance is at the sampling noise of 100k samples (about 0.002).
- **At T = 0.1 there is a consistent bias toward the global minimum.**
- **The shape inside each basin is not exact.** Mean f is 0.003–0.006 above the target at every T. The cause has not been tested.

### Why

- **Without T:** the minimizer of `E[f]` is a point mass on the global minimum.
  - Each batch, the samples in the worst basin have the most negative advantage, so that basin is drained first. As it drains, the remaining bad basins fall further below the batch mean.
  - Standardizing the advantage makes this faster, because as the distribution narrows even tiny f differences become unit-size advantages.
  - Once a basin gets no samples it gets no gradient, so it cannot come back.
- **With T:**
  - The optimum itself is multimodal.
  - The feedback is also reversed. If a basin loses mass, `log q` there drops, so its samples get a lower cost and pull mass back. Equilibrium is where `f_i + T·log q_i` is equal across basins, which is the Boltzmann ratio.
- **Limit:** the pull back only works while a basin is still sampled. When the target mass of a basin is small (1.8 % at T = 0.05, about 18 samples per batch), an early transient can empty it, and reverse KL does not rediscover lost modes.

**Rule of thumb (from these runs, batch 1024):** several minima survive when every basin's target mass stays well above 1/batch throughout training, which here means roughly Δf/T ≤ 2–3. Otherwise the distribution collapses to a single basin: the global one if Δf > 0, a random one if Δf = 0.

**Not tested yet:**
- whether the threshold moves with batch size (the explanation predicts that a larger batch tolerates lower T);
- whether it moves with basin width (all basins here have the same width).

## 3. TSP-20 with a fixed Boltzmann temperature

**Motivation:** the TSP-20 "RealNVP + KL" run standardizes the advantage and then adds `T·KL`. Relative to tour length, its effective temperature is therefore `T·std(length)`, which shrinks as the distribution concentrates, so it has no fixed Boltzmann target.

**Setup:** `permutation/tsp/realnvp_tsp20_boltzmann_trace.py` is a copy of `realnvp_tsp20_trace.py kl` with only the standardization removed (checked with diff). Its target is `P(tour) ∝ exp(−length/T)`, with T annealed from 0.1 to 0.001 in units of tour length. Argsort gives every permutation equal Gaussian mass, so this is the exact target over tours.

**Prediction before the run:** at T = 0.001, the near-optimal tour 3.522437 has relative weight e^−8.8 ≈ 1.6·10⁻⁴ against the optimum. So if the flow reaches the target, the final generator should sample the optimum on the seeds that found it.

| | RealNVP + KL (standardized) | Boltzmann (fixed T) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Runs that found the optimum | 8/10 | 9/10 |
| First hit, evaluations (min / median / max) | 403k / 542k / 799k | 403k / 496k / 806k |
| Fraction of test samples on the optimum | 0 on 10/10 | 0 on 10/10 |
| Best tour in test samples | 3.522437 on 10/10 | 3.522437 on 10/10 |
| Unique tours in 16384 test samples | 7–20 | 53–106 |
| Mean test length | 3.5229 | 3.5427 (worse on 10/10) |
| Median validation mean at epoch 1000 / 2000 | 3.536 / 3.523 | 3.705 / 3.541 |
| Best validation at the last epoch | 5/10 | 9/10 |

- **Hits:** seed 45 is gained. Seed 50 is missed by both. The first hit is at the same evaluation on seeds 43, 44 and 48, because the two runs are nearly identical early on (std(length) ≈ 1).
- **The prediction is refuted.** The optimum fraction is 0 on every seed.
- **The flow lags the temperature schedule.** It is still improving at the last epoch and never reaches the T = 0.001 target.
- **Interpretation:** near the end, the fixed temperature is much higher than the effective temperature of the standardized KL run. That buys diversity at the cost of a worse mean, but it does not move mass onto the optimum. Reverse KL keeps the modes the flow already samples; it does not relocate mass to a mode the flow is not sampling.
